CC4. Replication. Re-create, then improve, someone else's chart

Original chart

Source: Uppsala Conflict Data Program (2026); geoBoundaries (2023), processed by Our World in Data. CC BY 4.0.

This is the chart I took from Our World in Data, showing deaths in armed conflicts by region from 1989 to 2026. The issues I recognise are:

  1. The 2026 bar covers only the first half of the year but looks like deaths have collapsed.
  2. Stacking means only the bottom region shares a baseline, so it is hard to compare Europe, the Middle East and Asia.
  3. The tan and salmon colours are close in lightness, which can be hard for colour-blind readers.
  4. The title names the topic without stating a finding.

Replicated chart

I replicated the chart by downloading the CSV from Our World in Data and saving it to my GitHub repository. The dates did not need amending, as the data only contains years rather than full dates. I created a deaths field from the original Deaths in armed conflicts column using a calculate step, for simplicity. I then wrote a Vega-Lite JSON file in VS Code (cc4_replica.json) that loads the CSV, filters it to four regions, and tidies the region names. It uses a stacked bar mark, with Americas at the bottom, and I styled it to match the original: white background, serif title, dashed grid lines, alternate-year labels and similar colours. I embedded it with vegaEmbed.

Improved chart

I switched to lines for 2005 to 2025, replaced end labels with a legend, used colour-blind-safe colours, dropped incomplete 2026, and rewrote the title.

CC5. Accessing data: (1) API, (2) Scraper

API chart

Base URL: https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/GBR/indicator/FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG. api.worldbank.org/v2 is the World Bank API (version 2), country/GBR selects the United Kingdom, and indicator/FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG selects annual consumer price inflation (%).

Elements needed to call the data: a country code (GBR), an indicator code (FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG), and the options format=json and per_page=200, joined with ? and &. No API key is needed.

Data returned: JSON. The first item is a metadata block (page, total, last updated) and the second item is the list of observations, which is why the chart reads property: "1". The data is tidy: one row per year, with date (a text year, which the chart converts to a number) and value (the inflation rate). Some recent years are null, so the chart filters them out.

Full URL used in the chart: https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/GBR/indicator/FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG?format=json&per_page=200

CC7. Maps. Base maps and choropleths

Two maps of median gross weekly pay for full-time employees living in each place, April 2025 (ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings, downloaded through the Nomis API). The coordinates map shows 12 major cities in England, Scotland and Wales. The choropleth shades all 12 UK regions: the nine English regions plus Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Belfast is missing from the coordinates map because Northern Ireland's districts are not in the Nomis council list.

Google Colab notebook (download and cleaning)

Coordinates map

Seven of the eleven council-area cities pay less than their region or nation's average. Edinburgh, Aberdeen, Leeds and Newport pay more.

Choropleth map

Pay is highest in London (£903 a week) and lowest in the North East (£692). Scotland ranks fourth of 12, while Wales and Northern Ireland rank ninth and tenth.
